## Headline

**A defensible rule exists, and it is now registered in the fork as an opt-in bound:**
`Region.time_step_rule = 'realised_axis_minimum'` bounds the time step by

    dt <= courant_factor / (c * sqrt(sum over active axes of 1 / h_min(axis)**2))

where `h_min(axis)` is the smallest **realised** node spacing on that axis, read from
`Region.mesh_nodes`. The default, `'nominal'`, is unchanged and is not written to JSON.

- **C1 passes.** In SI arithmetic on the stored coordinates, the per-axis-minimum form reproduces the
  stored vendor `dt` of all **28** frozen P0-b resolutions to at most **`1.5404795319460567e-16`**
  relative. The registered gate is `2e-16`.
- **C2 passes.** On the registered graded fixture (realised x spacing `8.561643835616195` nm to
  `11.627906976750868` nm), the run at the opt-in bound completes all `18762` steps with finite
  fields and no post-source growth. A y-symmetry-breaking variant on the same nodes is also stable.
  The same variant at a Courant ratio of `1.05` diverges and is caught at step `600`.
- **The default path is bit-identical.** Frozen and changed solvers agree on 238 of 238 default-path
  values. The standing controls reproduce 443 of 443 non-timing readout leaves exactly. The fork
  differs from `$SOLVER_FROZEN` in exactly one of 2939 tracked files, `torchfdtd/models.py`.
- **The rule is a bound, not a selector claim (C4).** It is sufficient for the fork's vacuum Yee
  operator on these grids, with a measured spectral margin of `0.98987` to `0.99000` at the stored
  vendor `dt`. It says nothing about how the vendor chooses its constant. That mechanism stays `X`.

`M4` therefore does **not** have to close as uniform-only on account of `dt`. The rule's scope is
stated below, and so is what it does not cover.

## What the rule is, and why it is defensible

### The fork before this leg

`Region.cfl_time_step` took one step per axis from `Region.axis_steps`:

- `explicit` meshes: `axis_steps` is already the minimum realised difference of the coordinate tuple.
  So the default rule on an explicit mesh was already the per-axis-minimum rule.
- `uniform` and `graded` meshes: `axis_steps` is the configured `mesh_steps`, or `mesh` on every axis.
  That is the nominal step. On a graded mesh it is a statement about the mesher's fine lattice, not
  a reading of the realised mesh.

The fork's graded mesher (`torchfdtd/mesh.py`, `coarse_gap`) keeps refined intervals on the fine
lattice and refuses any coarse cell below `h * (1 - 1e-10)`. So the realised minimum differs from the
nominal step only by rounding and by that tolerance. On the fork-native graded case measured here,
the realised x minimum is `6.577493179745393e-14` relative **below** nominal. The nominal-rule `dt`
therefore **exceeds** the realised-minimum bound by `3.3084646133829665e-14` relative. That is far
inside the margin, but it is not zero, and until now nothing stated it.

### The change

`torchfdtd/models.py`, fork commit `ad436c930f2b4a87bcbfe7d87edc0abf49281ef1`, 20 lines added and 1 changed:

- new field `time_step_rule: Literal['nominal', 'realised_axis_minimum'] = 'nominal'`, dropped from
  the serialised dump while it is `'nominal'`, as `resident_cell_limit` already is. Default dumps,
  equality and hashes are therefore unchanged;
- new property `realised_axis_minima`, the minimum of `mesh_nodes[a][1:] - mesh_nodes[a][:-1]` per
  active axis;
- `cfl_time_step` uses those minima when the rule is opted in. Otherwise its code path is the old one;
- `rectangular_courant` skips its isotropic shortcut when the rule is opted in, so the Yee update
  coefficient is computed from the `dt` actually in use.

The existing override check (`time_step_override > cfl_time_step * (1 + 1e-12)` is refused) applies
unchanged. Under the opt-in rule it therefore refuses any override above the realised bound. The
`Project` validator re-runs it after `configure_auto_mesh`, so it is checked against the configured
mesh. FSP scene export refuses `region.time_step_rule` (an unmapped field), so it fails closed for
the opt-in rule and is unchanged for the default.

### Why it is a sufficient bound

The fork's metric curl (`torchfdtd/boundaries.py`, `YeeGrid._prepare_boundaries`) uses primal widths
`w_i` and dual widths `(w_i + w_{i+1}) / 2`; the periodic wrap dual is `(w_0 + w_last) / 2`. Every dual
width is therefore at least the axis minimum `h_min`. In the dual-weighted symmetric form of the 1-D
operator, Gershgorin gives a row sum of at most `4 / h_min**2`. On a rectilinear grid the vacuum 2-D
or 3-D operator is the sum of its axis operators. Leapfrog is stable when `c dt sqrt(lambda_max) / 2 <= 1`.
That holds whenever `dt <= 1 / (c sqrt(sum 1 / h_min**2))`, and `courant_factor <= 0.99` leaves a
further margin.

This argument is checked rather than trusted. The leg computes the exact largest eigenvalue of the
fork's operator on every frozen node set, for a Dirichlet truncation and for the fixture's x-PML /
y-periodic form, and reports `c dt sqrt(lambda_max) / 2` at the stored vendor `dt`. The range across
all 28 resolutions is **`0.9898737556565704` to `0.9899998881523209`**, all below `1`. The helper is
itself checked against the uniform closed forms in `checks/test_leg20_dt_rule.py`.

**Three things the table shows, which the record should carry:**

1. **The fork's own implementation does not meet `2e-16` on these nodes. It meets `8.053e-14`.** The
   fork works in micrometres. Converting each absolute coordinate (about 3 µm in magnitude) by
   `x * 1e6` rounds each one, and the difference of two rounded coordinates 2.14 nm apart carries
   that rounding relative to 2.14 nm. The worst case is the 160-ppw grids. C1 is registered on "the
   per-axis-minimum form" and is gated in SI on the stored coordinates, where it passes. The fork
   figure is reported, not gated. It is a units round-trip floor, not a different rule. In every
   case the fork's `dt` is the smaller of the two (for example `2.0003433003272522e-17` against
   `2.0003433003272664e-17`), and it sits inside a 1% spectral margin.
2. **These grids discriminate only weakly against the global-minimum form.** They are nearly
   isotropic (`dx_min` and `dy_min` differ by at most 0.114%), so the global-minimum form misses by
   only `1.262e-14` to `5.706e-04`. On the four batch-4 uniform resolutions, `dx = dy` and the two
   forms coincide up to rounding. At the `2e-16` tolerance the global-minimum form is still excluded
   on all 28. The strong exclusion (`0.269` to `0.388`) is REA's, on anisotropic vacuum layouts, and
   is cited, not re-run.
3. **On explicit meshes the fork's default already is this rule.** `fork_default_equals_opt_in` is
   `true` on all 28. The opt-in matters for `uniform` and `graded` meshes.

lossless slab across a periodic cell can trap guided energy. The measured final ratio of `8.1e-4`

`Simulation()` and in `plan.resolve_plan`. The script replaces `Region.cfl_time_step` with a property
returning infinity for those two runs only, restores it in `finally`, and asserts the restoration.
No solver file is involved.

## C3 — the limiter is an assumption, and what the bound rests on

The registered assumptions (`configs/leg20-dt-rule.json`, `assumptions`):

1. The bound is sufficient for the fork's **vacuum** Yee operator on a rectilinear grid whose dual
   widths are means of adjacent primal widths. Nondispersive `eps_r >= 1` with `mu_r = 1` only
   shrinks that operator. This is assumed; the n = 3.5 fixture is the only material case run.
2. Native CPML with `kappa >= 1` and `sigma >= 0` is assumed not to lower the limit. PEC and PMC wall
   factors are assumed covered by the image argument. Neither is proven here.
3. **A material, dispersive (ADE) or conformal stability limiter is not established in either
   project (`X`).** This bound does not establish one. Nothing here infers one, or its absence, from
   vacuum layouts.
4. The vendor selector mechanism is `X` and stays `X` (see C4).

**Which layouts the bound rests on:**

- **Vendor agreement:** 28 frozen 2-D resolutions in 11 files, all with an air background, one
  lossless nondispersive n = 3.5 slab (`lossless-slab-n3.5-d0.6`), x PML and y periodic: 20 staircase
  and 8 conformal-variant-0. They are **not** vacuum layouts, but they are one material family.
  They contain nothing 3-D, dispersive, multi-material, absorbing, or conformal-variant 1–2. The
  stored vendor `dt` on these 28 equals the geometric form. That shows no material limiter **acted**
  on these 28. It does not show that none exists.
- **Cited, not re-run:** REA `E02`, 14 of 14 vacuum layouts (graded and anisotropic 3-D included)
  at `<= 2.22e-16`.
- **Stability:** the exact spectral ratio on the vacuum operator of the 28 node sets and one
  fork-native graded mesh, plus two GPU runs (S1, S2) on one graded fixture with the n = 3.5 slab.
  No 3-D stability run, no dispersive material, and no subpixel interface (subpixel requires uniform
  spacing in the fork anyway).

## C4 — the rule is a bound, not a selector claim

The fork field comment and this record say the same thing. `realised_axis_minimum` **bounds** the
time step. It does not claim that the vendor selects its time step this way. The 28-of-28 agreement
is a measured constraint on the vendor's output (class `M`, as P0-b recorded it). The selector
mechanism remains `X` in both projects.
