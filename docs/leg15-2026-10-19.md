# Leg 15 evidence — attributing the `7.5e-7` closure residual — 2026-10-19

## Headline

**The `7.5e-07` residual of `T + R = 1` on the non-empty slab fixture is an echo of the absorbing
boundary (the PML) that returns through the structure and interferes at the two extraction planes.**
It is not a physical leak, not a solver defect, and not a discretisation floor.

Two laws, both measured:

1. **It is set by the PML thickness.** `1.36e-04` at 8 cells, `1.06e-05` at 16, `7.51e-07` at 32,
   `1.61e-07` at 48, `1.38e-07` at 50 — about `13x` per doubling at 8-32 cells, then slower. The
   single-interface fixture obeys the same knob: `8.05e-06`, `6.82e-07`, `4.89e-08`, `1.22e-08`,
   `1.08e-08`.
2. **It is linear in the reflected field amplitude.** With the interface fixture at three index
   contrasts, residual `/` `r` (where `r = (n-1)/(n+1)`) is constant to within `0.6 %` at a 32-cell
   PML (`8.800e-08`, `8.803e-08`, `8.902e-08`) and within `3 %` at a 50-cell PML. A power leak would
   scale with `r^2`, not `r`.

Everything that is *not* the boundary leaves the magnitude where it was: `ppw` 20, 40, 80 give
`7.539e-07`, `7.508e-07`, `7.380e-07`; duration 260 to 1000 fs gives `7.442e-07`; 4 to 16 micrometres
of padding and moving the slab 1.5 micrometres change it by 2-6 % (they shift the interference
pattern's phase, not its envelope); a 10x denser frequency grid leaves the maximum unchanged.

Two controls bound the claim. The no-interface null (slab index `1.0`) gives **exactly `0.0`**: with
no reflection there is no residual at all. The lossy control gives `3.40e-01`, `4.5e+05` times the
effect under test, so the measure is neither blind nor saturated.

The last row is the one honest outlier: a **1.2 micrometre** slab shows `1.69e-04` while its
composition gap stays at `9.2e-07`. A thicker cavity has more closely spaced resonances, and the
unapodized finite-window DFT on a 41-point wavelength grid evidently under-resolves that case. That
is a **different** mechanism from the boundary echo, it is **not** investigated here, and it is
reported rather than explained (stop condition 2: do not declare a mechanism from correlation).
Practical consequence, stated plainly: the closure residual is not one number across geometries; it
is `~1e-6`-or-better for the thin-slab fixtures this project uses and it degrades sharply for a
thicker cavity with this frequency grid.

## The decision this leg hands to the owner

Leg 13's standing `T + R = 1` control declares a `1e-06` floor on the slab case, derived from this
residual, and passes with a margin of `1.33x`. The floor is therefore **fixture-bound**, not
scheme-bound: it is set by the 32-cell PML of the fixture the control happens to use. Two options,
both cheap, neither taken here because the control's tolerance is a registered value:

* **A (no change).** Keep `1e-06` and record the attribution, so nobody later reads `1.33x` as a
  property of the scheme. The control keeps its tight-but-passing margin.
* **B (raise the PML of that fixture, 2 minutes).** `48` cells gives `1.61e-07` — a `6.2x` margin —
  and `50` gives `1.38e-07`, `7.2x`. This changes a registered control fixture, so it needs the
  owner's word.

`configs/leg13-standing-controls.json` now carries the corrected basis text (option A's bookkeeping);
the tolerance, the fixture and the archived run are untouched.

## What is not claimed

* The mechanism is established for **this fixture family** (a slab or a single interface between a
  one-way source and a PML, at `ppw` 20-80). Nothing here says what a PML echo does to a Bloch or a
  modal fixture, which have their own boundaries and their own sources.
* The thick-slab `1.69e-04` is **unexplained**, and the "under-resolved resonance" line above is a
  candidate, not a finding.
* The `13x`-per-doubling figure is a fit to six points at one `ppw`; the PML's own reflection law
  (a graded profile's super-exponential decay) was not derived here.
* No label changed, no number became `verified`, and the PML is not a defect: an absorbing boundary
  that reflects at `10^-6` is doing its job.
