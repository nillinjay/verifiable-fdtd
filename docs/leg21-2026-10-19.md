### 1. Modal eigenproblem and propagation must use the same derivative

`torchfdtd/mode_branches.py:131` requires a real uniform Yee-sampled region, including the exact text
`mesh_type != 'uniform'`. This is the required anchor from the registration. It does **not** inspect
derivative order: a higher-order derivative can still be uniform and Yee-staggered. Removing that guard
would not fix the actual operator mismatch.

The transverse two-point difference is visible at `torchfdtd/mode_ports.py:22` and used by the mode
solver at `:221`. The source preparation converts its longitudinal propagation parameter using
`beta = 2*np.arcsin(ratio)/h` at `torchfdtd/mode_branches.py:181`. That inverse sine matches the
nearest-neighbour spatial symbol. A new symbol generally needs a new inverse and propagation-band
test, and the transverse eigenproblem must match it. The temporal sine mapping can remain if the
time integration remains leapfrog; changing spatial order alone is not a reason to replace it.

**Repair cost:** one shared derivative/symbol specification; changes to transverse matrices and both
modal-preparation routes, including the invariant two-dimensional route; regenerated profiles, phase,
normalisation and calibration identities. The affected surfaces are named in the record. These are
work products, not a measured schedule estimate.

**Falsification (algebra within `next_scope` once a candidate is named):** substitute the unchanged
fields and propagation constant into the candidate's full
discrete Maxwell eigenproblem. A residual within a separately registered tolerance, over the claimed
band with the required normalisation, would refute the mismatch for that scope. Passing a mesh-type
guard or matching at a single zero-wavevector case would not establish this.

### 2. Modal source support must cover every coupling across its cut

`torchfdtd/mode_branches.py:210` and `:212` select one electric-field sheet and one magnetic-field
sheet. Line `:218` gives the electric forcing amplitude; `:189` checks only one aperture edge ring;
`:155` uses a fixed two-cell longitudinal clearance. A wider derivative can couple across a launch cut
from several rows, or reach past those transverse and longitudinal margins.

The exact algebraic question is the support of **`C M - M C`**, where `C` is the candidate discrete
curl and `M` is the mask selecting the launched side. Applying this commutator to the incident
eigenfield gives the forcing needed to cut that field. A nonzero entry outside the current sheets
cannot be supplied by changing only their amplitudes.

**Repair cost:** derive and store all cut-crossing current sheets with the correct electric/magnetic
half-step timing; derive the confinement ring and clearance from stencil reach; update source-byte
admission; check detector collocation and recalibrate. A second-order collar around the source is an
alternative, but adds an order-transition interface that must itself be assessed.

**Falsification (algebra within `next_scope` once a candidate is named):** if the commutator gives zero
on all omitted rows and matches the current packet on
its existing rows and times, the old support survives for that field. A later authorised propagation
check needs a separately authorised implementation and run to measure backward leakage and power
balance. A normalised transmitted-power
ratio can hide a common source error and is insufficient by itself.

### 3. CPML must filter the same complete derivative and have a valid closure

CPML means **convolutional perfectly matched layer**, the absorbing boundary with auxiliary memory.
`torchfdtd/boundaries.py:382` forms `field[high] - field[low]`. Line `:389` updates its auxiliary
memory from that derivative. The active-row range at `:318` and the wrap at `:397` assume one
neighbour. The fused implementation has the same target-memory recurrence at
`torchfdtd/cuda_kernels.py:201`.

Changing only the interior operator would leave incompatible entrance or terminal rows. Since the
stretching coefficients vary spatially, a local stretch and a nonlocal derivative cannot simply be
assumed to commute. This does not establish that the recurrence needs replacing: applying one
target-memory filter to the complete wider derivative may preserve its state count.

**Repair cost:** derive the stretched target operator, its entrance/corner/outer closures and halos;
match reference and fused derivative/filter ordering; reassess absorbing-layer thickness in physical
length after coarsening; validate reflection and late-time stability. Extra auxiliary memories remain
an open design question, not a declared cost multiplier.

**Falsification:** closure and augmented amplification can be assessed by algebra within `next_scope`
once a candidate is named. Derive it with the existing recurrence and layout, checking that every row
matches the stretched equations, all halo samples exist and the registered amplification condition
holds. Reflection and late-time decay require a separately authorised implementation and run with
registered accuracy criteria. Refuting the full concern needs both kinds of evidence. The bulk
derivative still has to change.

### 4. Coarsening must preserve interface accuracy and modal material agreement

The staggered sample locations are explicit at `torchfdtd/solver.py:40`; separate electric-component
sampling is at `:58`; geometry membership assigns a point value at `:107`. The electric update uses
local inverse permittivity at `torchfdtd/boundaries.py:427`. Modal sampling has corresponding
offsets at `torchfdtd/mode_ports.py:207`. Permittivity is the material coefficient relating the
electric field to stored electric energy in this lossless assessment.

Higher smooth-medium derivative order does not imply improved accuracy at a discontinuous staircase
interface. Doubling the spacing can change component ownership or erase a feature independently of
bulk dispersion. The retained risk is **the equal-accuracy/coarsening contract**, not a claim that
Yee locations or local positive material coefficients are universally invalid with higher order.

**Repair cost:** compare material ownership and feature representation with the existing sourced
geometry, keep modal and bulk material operators consistent, and either limit the claim to smooth
material or scope interface-aware closure/local refinement. Existing subpixel machinery is not
assumed to provide a compatible high-order interface treatment.

**Falsification:** before implementation, only an applicable cited published equal-output-error result
can refute this concern within `next_scope`; none is supplied here. Algebraic ownership checks cannot
establish output accuracy. A project comparison needs a separately authorised candidate implementation
and run, with exact sourced fixtures, interface-flux/output tolerances and a refined reference registered
beforehand. None of those inputs is named or registered here. Evidence must establish feature
preservation, modal/material agreement and the required output accuracy at twice the spacing without
interface repair. Homogeneous or precisely aligned examples cannot establish this for arbitrary interfaces.

### 5. The time-step admission bound must follow the actual operator spectrum

The CFL bound (Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy stability bound) at `torchfdtd/models.py:449` and `:451`
depends on spacing and active dimension, not new stencil coefficients. Overrides are compared with
that bound at `:484`. The realised-axis-minimum rule added in leg 20 remains a rule for the current
Yee operator; its proof is not automatically a proof for a wider derivative.

For an energy-compatible lossless periodic system with leapfrog time integration, let
`omega_max` be the largest discrete angular eigenfrequency in radians per second. The oscillatory
stability condition is `dt * omega_max < 2`, with a separately chosen safety margin. Here `dt` is
the time step in seconds. A new derivative can raise `omega_max` at the same spacing, but this is
not universal and the existing safety margin may suffice for a specific candidate.

**Repair cost:** obtain an operator/closure-specific bound and override rule, preserve the default,
and separately assess the augmented field-plus-CPML update. Recompute source and material data
that depend on time step. Periodic lossless eigenfrequencies alone do not settle CPML stability.

**Falsification (algebra within `next_scope` once a candidate and closure are named):** the complete
admitted candidate family fits within the existing step with the
required spectral margin, and its boundary amplification condition also passes. A short simulation
with only smooth low-frequency excitation cannot refute a highest-frequency stability concern.
Any later transient measurement needs a separately authorised implementation and run.

## Benefit, ranking and bounded follow-up

The registration's **"<= 8x at 2x coarser spacing"** has `provenance: "unverified"`.
Reading this as "fewer cells" is this assessment's interpretation, recorded in `interpretation`,
not part of the quotation or a sourced identification of what the factor measures. Under that reading,
the following cell-count arithmetic is conditional. It is not an established equal-error result.
For fixed physical extents, the interior count is
`N = product(L_i / h_i)`. Doubling spacing on `d` active axes divides the ideal count by `2**d`:
four in two dimensions and eight in three. This is arithmetic, not evidence that the coarser mesh
preserves the desired output. Integer rounding, physical PML thickness, fine collars, geometry
and fixed overhead alter total memory savings.

To expose the missing quantities, let `q` be the ratio of candidate to Yee maximum lossless
eigenfrequency at the same spacing in the same homogeneous medium, `w` the candidate time per
cell-step relative to Yee, and `m`
its stored bytes per interior cell relative to Yee. All three ratios are dimensionless. For a fixed
scale-invariant uniform stencil, equal stability margin and bulk-limited step, doubling spacing
gives a step ratio `2/q`. At fixed physical duration, the ideal speedup is then
`2**(d+1)/(q*w)` and interior memory reduction `2**d/m`. Boundary and setup costs are omitted from
these conditional formulas. None of `q`, `w`, `m` or equal-output accuracy was measured here.

The ranked options are:

1. **Further scoping:** resolve the candidate's algebra and equal-error evidence before buying the
   coupled production work. Recommended.
2. **Decline:** protect the forward, verification and inverse method critical path, but leave a
   potentially useful smooth-medium option unexplored.
3. **Register an implementation candidate:** start multiple coupled repairs now, risking that
   interface, boundary or time-step costs erase the nominal benefit. Evidence is insufficient.

The follow-up is one bounded evidence package: name a published candidate and its material/boundary
scope; derive its mode residual, cut-current support, CPML closure and spectral bound; attach a
source-backed equal-error comparison or identify exact existing fixtures and predeclare the output
tolerances for a separate authorised comparison. This record does not authorise that work or a fork
implementation. No physical parameters or performance thresholds are invented here.

Only advance to an implementation registration when all five contracts have either a concrete repair
or demonstrated non-breakage, and equal-output-error evidence supports **both** runtime and peak-memory
benefit after all overhead. Decline a scope if either benefit disappears. If the bounded package still
lacks these inputs, decline production adoption rather than extending scoping indefinitely. Actual
engineering time, equal-error coarsening, speedup, extra CPML storage and vendor semantics remain unknown.
Before implementation, interface accuracy can only be cleared by applicable cited published evidence.
Producing project evidence instead requires separate authorisation for experimental implementation
and comparison, with exact fixtures and tolerances registered first; this record grants none.
