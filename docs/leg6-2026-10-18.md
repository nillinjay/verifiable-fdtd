## The citation, quoted

> **R. Marchetti, C. Lacava, A. Khokhar, et al., "High-efficiency grating-couplers: demonstration of
> a new design strategy", Sci Rep 7, 16670 (2017), DOI `10.1038/s41598-017-16505-z`**
>
> Width: *"The grating was **12 µm wide** (along the x̂ direction of Fig. 1(left)), to properly
> accommodate the Gaussian mode of the fiber, and 14.847 µm long."*
>
> Fibre: *"The fiber, which was assumed to have an outer diameter of 125 µm and a **mode field
> diameter (MFD) of 10.4 µm**, was tilted by θ_air = 14.5°"*

That paper simulates its coupler with **2D-FDTD** in a `33.4 x 5.8` µm window — the same modelling
limitation this project has, and the reason the width has to be stated separately in both.

The waist is **derived, not quoted**: the overlap model uses `g = exp(-(z/w_lat)^2)`, a field
amplitude with 1/e radius `w_lat`, so `w_lat = MFD/2 = 5.2` µm. The leg labels it `derived` and
states the derivation; the report does not pretend the paper printed 5.2.

## The model's own limitation, stated

The aperture is **laterally uniform**, so it delivers power across its whole width, including where
the fibre mode is not. The overlap therefore has **one optimum** in width, near twice the waist, and
**falls on both sides**:

| `W` (µm) | 0.2 | 5 | 10 | **12 (cited)** | 20 | 100 | 1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `eta_lat` at `w_lat = 5.2` | `0.031` | `0.66` | `0.890` | **`0.875`** | `0.64` | `0.13` | `<0.02` |

**A wider device is not automatically a better one under this model.** The cited 12 µm sits within
`1.7 percent` of the model's peak factor, which is consistent with the paper's own stated reason for
that width ("to properly accommodate the Gaussian mode of the fiber").

## The total efficiency is an expression, not a number

`eta_total = eta_line_focus x eta_lat`, and `eta_line_focus` is the 2D line-focus efficiency of the
guided device. **It is not in this repository.** The reference project records `26.16 percent` on one
fixture and `45.95 percent` on another; those are that project's numbers for that project's
fixtures, and they are cited, not used.

For illustration only, with those two cited numbers and `eta_lat = 0.8745`:

| cited line-focus, per fixture | x `eta_lat` = illustrative total |
|---|---|
| `0.2616` | `0.2288` |
| `0.4595` | `0.4018` |

**Neither is this project's result.** They are arithmetic on somebody else's measurement, printed so
the size of the missing factor is visible. This project's own line-focus number requires its own run
and its own registration.

## What this leg does NOT establish

1. **It does not compute a fibre efficiency.** One factor is reported; the other is named as missing.
2. **The width is a borrow from a 260 nm SOI design.** The cited paper's `12 µm` grating is from its
   `260 nm` Si design (`e = 160 nm`, `R = 0.025 µm⁻¹`, CE `83 percent`). This project's device is not
   that device. The number is used because it is a *published width chosen for a single-mode fibre*,
   and it is labelled as such.
3. **The model is an aperture model, not a mode solver.** It assumes the device radiates uniformly
   across its width. A focused or transversely apodized coupler would need a different model; that is
   `S`-track work, not this leg.
4. **`Q3` remains answered by this leg's existence, not by a decision.** The platform now exposes the
   readout taking `(W, w_lat)` as arguments, which is what `Q3` asked for. The values are fixture
   parameters and are now cited rather than hand-supplied.
